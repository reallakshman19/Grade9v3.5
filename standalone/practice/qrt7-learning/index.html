Guided Lesson · SYNTHESIZE D1 Practise connecting steps
Target: Choose and connect a route from the combined system to an individual block; distinguish its net force from its contact force.

"A contact force and a block's net force can have different values. We will connect the parts and check which force belongs where."

[ 20 N push -> ] [ Block A: 2 kg ] [ Block B: 3 kg ]
Frictionless horizontal table · Blocks remain in contact.
Question: What contact force does B exert on A?
Stage 1: System Boundary
Circle A+B. The 20 N push comes from outside. The contact pair is internal to this combined system.
Choose the system you would start with and say what force remains external:
Feedback: For A+B the internal contact pair cancels in the combined sum, while the external 20 N push remains.
a = 20 / (2 + 3) = 4 m/s² right
Stage 2: Isolate Block B
Now isolate B. Its only horizontal force is A's contact force on B.
Use the common acceleration (4 m/s²) to find that contact force:
Feedback: For B: C = 3 × 4 = 12 N right. B's reciprocal force on A is 12 N left.
C = m_B a = 3 × 4 = 12 N
Stage 3: Distinguish Net Force vs Contact Force
Return to A: a 20 N push right and a 12 N contact force left.
Choose what 8 N describes:
Feedback: A's net force is 20 - 12 = 8 N right = 2 × 4. The contact force is 12 N; the net force is the sum of the forces received by A.
F_net,A = 20 - 12 = 8 N right
Stage 4: Reconstruct the Route
Worked equations hidden for recall.
Combined: a = 20 / (2+3) = 4 m/s²
Block B: C = 3 × 4 = 12 N right (reciprocal on A: 12 N left)
Block A net: F_net = 20 - 12 = 8 N right
Explain or reconstruct how you would get the contact force, and why it differs from A's net force. A sentence, sketch description or calculation is welcome. You can also choose "I cannot explain it yet."
Compare with: Use A+B to get their common acceleration, then isolate B to get its contact force, then reverse direction for the reciprocal force on A. An equivalent consistent route is valid.
