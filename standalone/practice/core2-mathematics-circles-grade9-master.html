Perpendicular Distance from Centre to Chord

Subtopic: Perpendicular from Centre to Chord
In a circle of radius $13\text{ cm}$, a chord $AB$ has length $24\text{ cm}$. Find the perpendicular distance of the chord from the centre $O$ of the circle.
Vector Figure: FIG-IMO-G09-CIRCLES-Q001.svg
Trap Analysis: Forgetting that the perpendicular bisects the chord, attempting to use the full chord length 24 cm in Pythagoras.
Subtracted $13 - 12 = 1$ or arithmetic error.
Correct distance derived from the fundamental Pythagorean circle bisector theorem.
Confused distance with diameter quotient $24 / 4 = 6$.
Mistook $(13 - 6) = 7$ as the perpendicular leg.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Drop a perpendicular $OM$ from centre $O$ to chord $AB$. What does $OM$ do to segment $AB$?
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
The perpendicular bisects $AB$, so $AM = MB = \frac{24}{2} = 12\text{ cm}$. In $\triangle OMA$, hypotenuse $OA = 13\text{ cm}$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
The perpendicular from the circle centre to a chord bisects the chord, forming a right triangle with the radius.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: B ($5\text{ cm}$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. Let $OM \perp AB$ where $M$ is the foot of the perpendicular on $AB$. 2. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord: $AM = \frac{1}{2} AB = \frac{24}{2} = 12\text{ cm}$. 3. Connect $O$ to $A$ to form right-angled $\triangle OMA$ with hypotenuse $OA = r = 13\text{ cm}$. 4. By the Pythagorean theorem: $OM^2 + AM^2 = OA^2 \implies OM^2 + 12^2 = 13^2$. 5. $OM^2 = 169 - 144 = 25 \implies OM = 5\text{ cm}$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
Check chord length from distance: $AB = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2} = 2\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 2\sqrt{169 - 25} = 2\sqrt{144} = 2 \times 12 = 24\text{ cm}$. Matches stem exactly.

Subtended Angle by Chord of Length Equal to Radius

Subtopic: Central vs Inscribed Angle
A chord of a circle is equal to the radius of the circle. Find the angle subtended by the chord at the centre of the circle, and the angle subtended by it at a point on the major arc.
Trap Analysis: Confusing the major arc inscribed angle ($30^\circ$) with the minor arc inscribed angle ($150^\circ$).
Correct: $60^\circ$ at the centre and $30^\circ$ on the major arc.
Assumed the inscribed angle equals the central angle.
Assumed the chord subtends a right angle at the centre.
Doubled the central angle instead of halving it for the inscribed angle.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Connect both ends of the chord to the circle's centre. What type of triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord?
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
All three sides equal $r$, so the central angle is $60^\circ$. The angle subtended at the circumference is half the central angle.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
A chord equal in length to the radius forms an equilateral triangle with the center, subtending 60 degrees at the center and 30 degrees at the circumference.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: A ($60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. Let the chord be $AB = r$, and $O$ be the centre of the circle. Radii $OA = OB = r$. 2. In $\triangle OAB$, $OA = OB = AB = r$, so $\triangle OAB$ is an equilateral triangle. 3. Therefore, the angle subtended at the centre is $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$. 4. By the Inscribed Angle Theorem, the angle subtended by an arc at the circumference is half the angle subtended at the centre. 5. For any point $P$ on the major arc: $\angle APB = \frac{1}{2} \angle AOB = \frac{1}{2}(60^\circ) = 30^\circ$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
The inscribed angle on the minor arc would be $180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$. Reflex $\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 60^\circ = 300^\circ$, and $\frac{1}{2}(300^\circ) = 150^\circ$, confirming consistency.

Vector Figure: FIG-IMO-G09-CIRCLES-Q003.svg
Trap Analysis: Failing to recognize that $\angle BAC$ and $\angle BDC$ share the identical chord base $BC$.
Subtracted $130^\circ - (20^\circ + 10^\circ) = 100^\circ$ through arithmetic slip.
Correct angle $110^\circ$ derived from segment angle invariance.
Calculated $180^\circ - 130^\circ + 70^\circ = 120^\circ$.
Mistook the vertically opposite or linear pair angle directly.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Find $\angle DEC$ along the straight line segment $BD$, then compute $\angle CDE$ in $\triangle CDE$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
$\angle DEC = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$. Then $\angle CDE = 180^\circ - (50^\circ + 20^\circ) = 110^\circ$. Notice that $\angle BAC$ and $\angle BDC$ subtend the same arc $BC$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
Angles subtended by the same chord in the same segment of a circle are equal.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: B ($110^\circ$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. Points $B, E, D$ lie on a straight line. Therefore: $\angle DEC = 180^\circ - \angle BEC = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$. 2. In $\triangle DEC$, the sum of interior angles is $180^\circ$: $\angle CDE = 180^\circ - (\angle DEC + \angle ECD) = 180^\circ - (50^\circ + 20^\circ) = 110^\circ$. 3. Note that $\angle BDC$ is the exact same angle as $\angle CDE$, which subtends arc $BC$. 4. By the Inscribed Angle Theorem, angles in the same segment of a circle are equal: $\angle BAC = \angle BDC = 110^\circ$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
In $\triangle CDE$, exterior angle $\angle BEC = \angle CDE + \angle ECD = 110^\circ + 20^\circ = 130^\circ$. Verified identically.

Subtracted $38^\circ$ from $86^\circ$ or arithmetic error.
Correct complementary angle $52^\circ$.

Added $38^\circ$ incorrectly or subtracted from $100^\circ$.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux

What is the measure of the angle subtended by a diameter in a semicircle?

$\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. The sum of angles in $\triangle ABC$ is $180^\circ$.

The angle subtended by a diameter at any point on the circle is a right angle (90 degrees).

$38^\circ + 90^\circ + 52^\circ = 180^\circ$. Satisfies triangle angle sum theorem exactly.

Concentric Circle Intercepted Segment Invariant

Subtopic: Concentric Circles Intercepted Chords
Two concentric circles with common centre $O$ have radii $17\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$. A straight line intersects the outer circle at $A$ and $D$, and the inner circle at $B$ and $C$. If the perpendicular distance from $O$ to the line is $8\text{ cm}$, find the length of the line segment $AB$.
Trap Analysis: Subtracting radii directly ($17 - 10 = 7$) instead of computing the Pythagorean chord projections.
Calculated $17 - 10 = 7\text{ cm}$ (difference of radii).
Used the distance $8\text{ cm}$ directly.
Correct segment length $9\text{ cm}$ from dual chord bisection.
Arithmetic error calculating $16 - 6 = 10\text{ cm}$.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Draw a perpendicular $OM$ from $O$ to the line $AD$. $M$ is the midpoint of both $AD$ and $BC$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
Compute half-chord $AM = \sqrt{17^2 - 8^2}$ and half-chord $BM = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2}$. Then $AB = AM - BM$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
A perpendicular from the common centre to a secant line bisects both the outer chord and the inner chord simultaneously.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: C ($9\text{ cm}$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. Let $OM \perp AD$ with $OM = 8\text{ cm}$. 2. In the outer circle of radius $R = 17\text{ cm}$, $M$ bisects chord $AD$. In right $\triangle OMA$: $AM = \sqrt{OA^2 - OM^2} = \sqrt{17^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{289 - 64} = \sqrt{225} = 15\text{ cm}$. 3. In the inner circle of radius $r = 10\text{ cm}$, $M$ bisects chord $BC$. In right $\triangle OMB$: $BM = \sqrt{OB^2 - OM^2} = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} = 6\text{ cm}$. 4. Segment $AB = AM - BM = 15 - 6 = 9\text{ cm}$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
By symmetry, $CD = DM - CM = 15 - 6 = 9\text{ cm} = AB$. Total chord $AD = AB + BC + CD = 9 + 12 + 9 = 30\text{ cm} = 2 \times 15\text{ cm}$. Exact match.

Trap Analysis: Selecting the central angle $150^\circ$ directly, or finding the opposite inscribed angle $105^\circ$.
Correct: $75^\circ$ via half of central angle $150^\circ$.
Calculated $\frac{170^\circ}{2} = 85^\circ$ through arithmetic slip.
Calculated the inscribed angle at $B$ ($105^\circ$) instead of the angle at $D$.
Gave the central angle $150^\circ$ without dividing by 2.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux

Find the central angle $\angle AOC$ subtended by minor arc $AC$ by subtracting the other two central angles from $360^\circ$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
$\angle AOC = 360^\circ - (90^\circ + 120^\circ) = 150^\circ$. Point $D$ lies on the opposite arc, so $\angle ADC = \frac{1}{2} \angle AOC$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
Complete the circular angular sum around the centre, then apply the inscribed angle theorem to the remaining arc.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: A ($75^\circ$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. The sum of all central angles around centre $O$ is $360^\circ$: $\angle AOB + \angle BOC + \angle AOC = 360^\circ$. 2. Minor arc $AC$ subtends central angle $\angle AOC = 150^\circ$. 4. Point $D$ is on the major arc $AC$. By the Inscribed Angle Theorem, the angle subtended by an arc at any point on the remaining part of the circle is half the central angle: $\angle ADC = \frac{1}{2}\angle AOC = \frac{1}{2}(150^\circ) = 75^\circ$.

The central angle for major arc $AC$ is reflex $\angle AOC = 210^\circ$, so inscribed angle at $B$ is $\angle ABC = \frac{210^\circ}{2} = 105^\circ$. In cyclic quad $ABCD$, opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$: $\angle ADC + \angle ABC = 75^\circ + 105^\circ = 180^\circ$. Verified.

Cyclic Quadrilateral Opposite Angle Bisectors Invariant

Subtopic: Cyclic Quadrilateral Angle Bisectors
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral. The internal angle bisectors of opposite angles $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ intersect the circumcircle at points $P$ and $Q$ respectively. What can be concluded about the line segment $PQ$?
Trap Analysis: Guessing parallelism to diagonals rather than using the $90^\circ$ diameter invariant.
Assumed chord length equals radius $R$ instead of $2R$.
Correct: $PQ$ is a diameter because the inscribed angle subtending it is $90^\circ$.
Parallelism to $BD$ only holds in special symmetric configurations.
Perpendicularity to $AC$ does not hold in asymmetric cyclic quads.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Express the measure of arc $PQ$ in terms of the angle bisectors of $\angle A$ and $\angle C$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
$\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ \implies \frac{1}{2}\angle A + \frac{1}{2}\angle C = 90^\circ$. An inscribed angle of $90^\circ$ subtends a diameter.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
Since opposite angles of a cyclic quad sum to 180 degrees, the sum of their half-angles is 90 degrees, forcing the line joining the arc midpoints to be a diameter.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: B ($PQ$ is a diameter of the circle)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. In cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, opposite angles are supplementary: $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$. 2. Dividing by 2: $\frac{1}{2}\angle A + \frac{1}{2}\angle C = 90^\circ$. 3. The bisector of $\angle A$ meets the circle at $P$, so arc $BP = \text{arc } PD$, meaning $\angle BAP = \angle DAP = \frac{1}{2}\angle A$. 4. The bisector of $\angle C$ meets the circle at $Q$, so arc $DQ = \text{arc } QC$, meaning $\angle DCQ = \angle BCQ = \frac{1}{2}\angle C$. 5. Connecting $P$ and $Q$ through a point on the circumference yields an inscribed angle subtended by chord $PQ$ equal to $\frac{1}{2}\angle A + \frac{1}{2}\angle C = 90^\circ$. 6. By Thales theorem, an angle inscribed in a semicircle is $90^\circ$, which implies chord $PQ$ subtends a semicircle and is strictly a diameter of the circle.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
Let $ABCD$ be a square (a cyclic quad with all angles $90^\circ$). Angle bisectors pass through the centre $O$, and $P, Q$ are diametrically opposite vertices. Thus $PQ$ is a diameter.

Intersecting Chords Segment Product Theorem

Subtopic: Intersecting Chords Theorem
In the given figure, two chords $AB$ and $CD$ of a circle intersect at an interior point $P$. If $AP = 6\text{ cm}$, $PB = 8\text{ cm}$, and $CP = 4\text{ cm}$, find the total length of chord $CD$.
Vector Figure: FIG-IMO-G09-CIRCLES-Q008.svg
Trap Analysis: Reporting the segment $PD = 12\text{ cm}$ instead of the full chord length $CD = 16\text{ cm}$.
Added $AP + PB = 14\text{ cm}$ instead of finding $CD$.
Subtracted $PB - CP + AP = 8 - 4 + 6 = 10$ and added 5.
Correct total chord length $CD = 4 + 12 = 16\text{ cm}$.
Added $6 + 12 = 18$ mistakenly combining $AP$ and $PD$.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Apply the Intersecting Chords Theorem: $AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
Compute segment $PD = \frac{AP \cdot PB}{CP} = \frac{6 \times 8}{4} = 12\text{ cm}$. Remember to add $CP$ to find the total chord length $CD$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
For two chords intersecting inside a circle, the product of the segments of one chord equals the product of the segments of the other.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: C ($16\text{ cm}$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. In right and similar triangles formed by connecting chords $AC$ and $BD$: $\triangle APC \sim \triangle DPB$ (by AA similarity since $\angle PAC = \angle PDB$ in the same segment and vertically opposite angles at $P$ are equal). 2. Corresponding sides are in proportion: $\frac{AP}{PD} = \frac{CP}{PB} \implies AP \cdot PB = CP \cdot PD$. 3. Substitute the given values: $6 \times 8 = 4 \times PD \implies 48 = 4 PD \implies PD = 12\text{ cm}$. 4. The total length of chord $CD$ is $CP + PD = 4 + 12 = 16\text{ cm}$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
Product of segments: $AP \cdot PB = 6 \times 8 = 48$. $CP \cdot PD = 4 \times 12 = 48$. Equal products verify the Intersecting Chords Theorem.

Subtopic: Parallel Chords on Opposite Sides of Centre
In a circle of radius $10\text{ cm}$, two parallel chords $AB$ and $CD$ of lengths $16\text{ cm}$ and $12\text{ cm}$ respectively lie on opposite sides of the centre $O$. Find the perpendicular distance between the two chords.
Trap Analysis: Subtracting distances ($8 - 6 = 2$) instead of adding them when chords lie on opposite sides of the centre.
Calculated the distance if the chords were on the same side of the centre ($8 - 6 = 2\text{ cm}$).
Guessed the radius length $10\text{ cm}$.
Correct distance $14\text{ cm}$ from summing perpendicular distances across centre.
Mistakenly used the chord length $16\text{ cm}$.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Draw perpendiculars from centre $O$ to both chords. Half-lengths are $8\text{ cm}$ and $6\text{ cm}$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
Compute distances $d_1 = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = 6\text{ cm}$ and $d_2 = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 8\text{ cm}$. Since they lie on opposite sides, add the distances.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
When two parallel chords lie on opposite sides of the centre, the distance between them is the sum of their individual distances from the centre.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: C ($14\text{ cm}$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. Let $OM \perp AB$ and $ON \perp CD$. Since $AB \parallel CD$, the points $M, O, N$ are collinear. 2. In right $\triangle OMB$: $MB = \frac{16}{2} = 8\text{ cm}$, hypotenuse $OB = 10\text{ cm}$. $OM = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} = 6\text{ cm}$. 3. In right $\triangle OND$: $ND = \frac{12}{2} = 6\text{ cm}$, hypotenuse $OD = 10\text{ cm}$. $ON = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{100 - 36} = \sqrt{64} = 8\text{ cm}$. 4. Because the chords lie on opposite sides of the centre $O$, the total distance between them is $MN = OM + ON = 6 + 8 = 14\text{ cm}$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
If the chords were on the same side of the centre, the distance would be $ON - OM = 8 - 6 = 2\text{ cm}$. On opposite sides, it must strictly be $8 + 6 = 14\text{ cm}$.

Common Chord of Circles Passing Through Each Other's Centres

Subtopic: Common Chord of Intersecting Circles
Two circles of equal radius $5\text{ cm}$ intersect each other such that each circle passes through the centre of the other. Find the exact length of their common chord.
Trap Analysis: Confusing the equilateral triangle altitude formula with right-isosceles triangle geometry.
Assumed the chord length equals the radius $5\text{ cm}$.
Used diagonal factor $\sqrt{2}$ instead of equilateral height factor $\sqrt{3}$.
Correct chord length $5\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$ from equilateral triangle altitude.
Added the two radii $5 + 5 = 10\text{ cm}$.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Distance between centres $O_1$ and $O_2$ is equal to radius $R = 5\text{ cm}$. The common chord $AB$ perpendicularly bisects segment $O_1 O_2$.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
Let $M$ be the midpoint of $O_1 O_2$, so $O_1 M = 2.5\text{ cm}$. In $\triangle O_1 M A$, hypotenuse $O_1 A = 5\text{ cm}$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
When two equal circles pass through each other's centres, the line of centres equals the radius and is bisected by the common chord.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: C ($5\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$)
Step-by-Step Derivation
1. Let the centres be $O_1$ and $O_2$. Since each passes through the other's centre, the distance between centres is $O_1 O_2 = R = 5\text{ cm}$. 2. The line of centres $O_1 O_2$ is the perpendicular bisector of the common chord $AB$. 3. Let $M$ be the intersection of $O_1 O_2$ and $AB$. Then $O_1 M = \frac{1}{2} O_1 O_2 = \frac{5}{2} = 2.5\text{ cm}$. 4. In right-angled $\triangle O_1 M A$, hypotenuse $O_1 A = R = 5\text{ cm}$. 5. By Pythagoras theorem: $AM = \sqrt{O_1 A^2 - O_1 M^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - (2.5)^2} = \sqrt{25 - 6.25} = \sqrt{18.75} = \sqrt{\frac{75}{4}} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}\text{ cm}$. 6. The total length of the common chord is $AB = 2 \times AM = 2 \times \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$.
Independent Cross-Verification Check
$\triangle O_1 O_2 A$ has side lengths $O_1 O_2 = 5$, $O_1 A = 5$, $O_2 A = 5$, so it is an equilateral triangle of side $s = 5\text{ cm}$. The altitude $AM = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} s = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2}$. Thus $AB = 2 AM = 5\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$.

Subtopic: Cyclic Trapezoid Symmetry
Quadrilateral $ABCD$ is inscribed in a circle such that $AB \parallel CD$. If $\angle A = 70^\circ$, find the measures of $\angle B, \angle C$, and $\angle D$.

Swapped $\angle B$ and $\angle C$, confusing adjacent angles with opposite angles.
Correct angle triad: $\angle B = 70^\circ, \angle C = 110^\circ, \angle D = 110^\circ$.
Made $\angle C = 70^\circ$, violating the cyclic quad supplementary condition.
Inverted all three supplementary angles.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge
Use the cyclic quadrilateral opposite angle property (sum to 180 degrees) alongside parallel line consecutive interior angle properties.
Tier 2 · Tactical Step
$\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ \implies \angle C = 110^\circ$. Since $AB \parallel CD$, consecutive interior angles along $AD$ satisfy $\angle A + \angle D = 180^\circ \implies \angle D = 110^\circ$.
Tier 3 · Crux Move
An inscribed trapezoid in a circle is necessarily isosceles, forcing non-parallel sides to be equal and base angles to be equal.
Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: B ($\angle B = 70^\circ, \angle C = 110^\circ, \angle D = 110^\circ$)

Since $AB \parallel CD$ and $AD$ is a transversal, consecutive interior angles on the same side sum to $180^\circ$: $\angle A + \angle D = 180^\circ \implies 70^\circ + \angle D = 180^\circ \implies \angle D = 110^\circ$. 3. Again by cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles: $\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ$. $\angle B + 110^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \angle B = 70^\circ$. 4. Thus $\angle B = 70^\circ, \angle C = 110^\circ, \angle D = 110^\circ$ (an isosceles trapezoid with base angles equal).
Independent Cross-Verification Check
Sum of all four angles: $70^\circ + 70^\circ + 110^\circ + 110^\circ = 360^\circ$. Perfectly consistent with Euclidean polygon angle sums.

Step-by-Step Derivation
1. (Note that Euler's inequality $R \ge 2r$ is satisfied since $10 \ge 2 \times 4 = 8$). 3.
