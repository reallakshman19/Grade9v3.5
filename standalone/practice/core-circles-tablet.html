3 · Stage 2: Active Student Attempt

State the exact chain of two angle equalities connecting \(\angle ABF\) to \(\angle DEF\) and to \(\angle CDE\), and state why they force \(EF \parallel DC\):

💡 Need a Nudge? (Hint 1: Conceptual Invariant)
Vertices \(A\) and \(B\) lie on both circles. Look at the angle at vertex \(B\) (\(\angle ABF\)). How does it relate to the exterior angle at \(E\) in the inner circle, and how does it relate to the opposite angle at \(D\) in the outer circle?
🎯 Need Tactical Help? (Hint 2: Formulating Intermediate Step)
Write: (1) \(\angle DEF = \angle ABF\) (exterior angle equals interior opposite in quad \(ABFE\)).
Write: (2) \(\angle ADC + \angle ABF = 180^\circ\) (opposite angles in cyclic quad \(ABCD\)).
Substitute equation (1) into equation (2) to find \(\angle ADC + \angle DEF\).
4 · Stage 3: Reverse Reconstruction (Symptom to Root Cause)
Model Closure (Formal Mathematical Warrant):
1. In inner circle \(C_2\): \(ABFE\) is cyclic \(\implies \angle DEF = \angle ABF\).
2. In outer circle \(C_1\): \(ABCD\) is cyclic \(\implies \angle CDE = 180^\circ - \angle ABF\).
3. Sum: \(\angle DEF + \angle CDE = \angle ABF + (180^\circ - \angle ABF) = 180^\circ\).
4. Since consecutive interior angles on the same side of transversal \(AD\) sum to \(180^\circ\), \(EF \parallel DC\).
Why Reconstruction Differs from Teaching Path:
Teaching starts from circle axioms and moves forward. In problem solving, you work backward from the symptom: To prove \(EF \parallel DC\), what angle sum do we need? We need supplementary interior angles along \(AD\). What shared vertex connects both circles? Vertex \(B\)!

5 · Stage 4: Boundary & Perturbation Test

Suppose the inner circle is continuously dilated until it coincides identically with the outer circle. What happens to chord \(EF\)?

Correct! In the limit as \(C_2 \to C_1\), \(E \to D\) and \(F \to C\). The continuous deformation preserves the angle invariant across the entire family of circles.
