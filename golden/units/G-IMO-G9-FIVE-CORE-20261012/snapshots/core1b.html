Predict

A student checks t=3,4,5 and claims that (t−2)(t−1)t is divisible by 6 for every integer t≥3. Do those three checks PROVE the word every? Begin your written response with yes or no and explain what would count as evidence for every admissible t; then construct the general proof in the same response. If using a printed handout, write on a separate sheet.

Attempt

With t any integer at least 3, reconstruct a proof that (t−2)(t−1)t is divisible by 6. Do NOT list sample products as your justification. Show what forces a 2, what forces a 3, and why the two facts combine.

  • t is an arbitrary integer and t≥3.
  • The three factors are consecutive and positive.
  • A universally quantified statement must cover every admissible start.
Reconstruct

Boundary test

Suppose the claim changes to only TWO consecutive positive factors, (t−1)t, always being divisible by 6 for all integers t≥2. Is this universal claim true? Identify which guarantee from the three-factor proof fails. Record your boundary decision and reason before checking its answer. On a printed handout, write on a separate sheet.
