Which geometry model answers the question?

Distinguish a 2D ground footprint from a 3D enclosed capacity. This is an independently authored concept lesson; it does not reproduce the organizer’s sample Q008.

Concept before formulas

Decide the measurement dimension

Footprint → area (m²)Enclosed space → volume (m³)A model is justified by the quantity asked for and the shape's assumptions.

The same conical object may require more than one geometric model. Area counts a surface; volume counts space. Do not choose a familiar formula simply because the word cone appears.

Why valid: A circular floor has model A = πr², independent of height. A right circular cone has V = ⅓πr²h. The first is squared length; the second is cubed length.
Construction unit 01 · classify the question

Separate footprint from capacity

01

Name the quantity. Ground covering measures a region of the base; air capacity measures the entire solid’s space.

02

Check geometry assumptions. The base must be circular, and the capacity model must be a right circular cone (not a cylinder).

Stage 1 of 3

Authored worked anchor · changed measurements

A conical display has radius 2 m and height 6 m. The circular covering requires 4π m²; the enclosed capacity requires 8π m³.

Design targetChosen modelWorked result
Ground coverCircle A = πr²4π m²
Enclosed airCone V = ⅓πr²h8π m³

Both models use the same r = 2, but only volume needs h = 6; their units cannot be interchanged.

Construction unit 02 · compare candidates and counterfactuals

Reject a model for the right reason

03

Discriminate models. For a tapered cone, cylinder volume πr²h is three times the cone capacity: the shapes are not interchangeable.

04

Test a changed condition. With fixed radius, doubling height changes capacity but not footprint area.

Stage 1 of 3
Quick check · does the height affect the footprint?
No. A circular base’s area is πr²; only changing its radius changes its footprint.
Mistake clinic · treating the cone as a cylinder
For the same radius and height, the cylinder and right cone have different volume models. Inspect whether the shape tapers to an apex before choosing πr²h rather than ⅓πr²h.
Mistake clinic · confusing area with volume
Check units first: m² describes a surface, m³ describes enclosed space. The numerical values might coincidentally match but the two measures do not become the same physical quantity.
Independent exit · authored new situation

Choose both models on new values

A different conical shelter has radius 5 m and vertical height 9 m. Work out (i) its floor footprint and (ii) enclosed air volume. State which formula applies to each and explain why a cylinder model would be inappropriate.

Model reasoning · authored exit

Ground: A = π·5² = 25π m². Enclosed air: V = ⅓π·5²·9 = 75π m³. The cylinder's 225π m³ would overestimate the capacity because the cone tapers to its apex. The ground footprint does not require height.
