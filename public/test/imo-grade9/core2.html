Rigorous modular construction exhausting parity and modulo-3 residue classes.

Declared Conventions and Hypotheses

  • Domain: Positive integers $\mathbb{Z}^+$.
  • Consecutive Integers: $n, n+1, n+2$ for arbitrary start $n$.
  • Divisibility: $d \mid N \iff \exists k \in \mathbb{Z}: N = dk$.
  • Coprime Factorization: If $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, then $a \mid N \land b \mid N \iff ab \mid N$.
Explicit Inferential Jump:

Why testing particular starting integers is fundamentally insufficient, and how partitioning arbitrary $n$ across the complete residue classes modulo 2 and modulo 3 guarantees that at least one factor is even and one factor is a multiple of 3, synthesizing divisibility by 6 via coprimality $\gcd(2, 3) = 1$.

Universal Divisibility Theorems & Step-by-Step Proof

STEP 1 · ARBITRARY START

Let $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ be an arbitrary starting integer. The product is $P(n) = n(n+1)(n+2)$.

Why valid: Universal generalization over $\mathbb{Z}^+$ guarantees conclusions apply to all positive integers.

STEP 2 · PARITY LEMMA

In any pair of consecutive integers $\{n, n+1\}$, exactly one is even. Thus $2 \mid n(n+1) \implies 2 \mid P(n)$.

Why valid: Integers strictly alternate parity modulo 2.

STEP 3 · MODULO 3 EXHAUSTION

By division algorithm, $n \equiv 0, 1,$ or $2 \pmod 3$. If $n \equiv 0$, $3 \mid n$. If $n \equiv 1$, $3 \mid (n+2)$. If $n \equiv 2$, $3 \mid (n+1)$. In every case, $3 \mid P(n)$.

Why valid: Division algorithm guarantees that $\{0, 1, 2\}$ is a complete residue system modulo 3.

STEP 4 · COPRIME SYNTHESIS

Since $2 \mid P(n)$ and $3 \mid P(n)$, and $\gcd(2, 3) = 1$, by Euclid's lemma $2 imes 3 = 6 \mid P(n)$ for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$.

Why valid: Coprime divisors multiply to divide the product (Euclid's Lemma).

Core 1A · Universal Proof: Parallelogram Interior Angle Bisector Enclosure Theorem

Anchored to Quadrilaterals and Geometric Deductions (Q32 2024-25 Set B, Q30 2025-26 Set B, Q14 2025-26 Set A).

Declared Conventions and Domain

  • Domain: Euclidean Plane Geometry ($\mathbb{R}^2$).
  • Hypotheses: $ABCD$ is a non-degenerate parallelogram with $AB \parallel CD$ and $AD \parallel BC$.
  • Bisectors: Rays $AP, BP, CQ, DQ$ bisect interior angles $ngle A, ngle B, ngle C, ngle D$ respectively.
  • Enclosure: The four angle bisectors intersect to form an enclosed quadrilateral $PQRS$.
Explicit Inferential Jump:

Why the consecutive interior angles of any parallelogram sum to 180 degrees, forcing their bisected halves to sum to exactly 90 degrees, and how triangle angle sum transfers this invariant to prove that all four interior angles of the enclosed quadrilateral are strictly 90 degrees, forming a rectangle.

Formal 4-Stage Step-by-Step Construction Path

STEP 1 · CONSECUTIVE ANGLES

Since $AB \parallel CD$ with transversal $AD$, consecutive interior angles sum to 180 degrees: $ngle A + ngle D = 180^\circ$ and $ngle A + ngle B = 180^\circ$.

Why valid: Euclid's Parallel Postulate directly warrants that consecutive interior angles across parallel lines sum to two right angles ($180^\circ$).

STEP 2 · BISECTION SUM

Ray $AP$ bisects $ngle A$ and ray $DP$ bisects $ngle D$. In $ riangle APD$, the sum of base angles is: $ngle PAD + ngle PDA = rac{1}{2}(ngle A + ngle D) = rac{1}{2}(180^\circ) = 90^\circ$.

Why valid: Multiplying an algebraic equality by scalar factor $1/2$ preserves exact mathematical equivalence over $\mathbb{R}$.

STEP 3 · TRIANGLE APEX ANGLE

In $ riangle APD$, triangle angle sum gives $ngle APD = 180^\circ - (ngle PAD + ngle PDA) = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$.

Why valid: The angle sum property of any Euclidean triangle guarantees that the three interior angles sum to $180^\circ$.

STEP 4 · RECTANGLE INVARIANT

By identical symmetry for all consecutive angle pairs, the four interior angles at $P, Q, R, S$ are right angles ($90^\circ$). Vertically opposite angles transfer $90^\circ$ to quadrilateral $PQRS$, proving $PQRS$ is a rectangle.

Why valid: A quadrilateral possessing four right angles satisfies the definitive invariant of a Euclidean rectangle.
