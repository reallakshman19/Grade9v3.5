Perpendicular Distance from Centre to Chord

Subtopic: Perpendicular from Centre to Chord
In a circle of radius $13\text{ cm}$, a chord $AB$ has length $24\text{ cm}$. Find the perpendicular distance of the chord from the centre $O$ of the circle.
Vector Figure: FIG-IMO-G09-CIRCLES-Q001.svg
Commit Initial Reasoning & Working (Prior to Assistance)
Tempting Mistake & Why It Fails: Forgetting that the perpendicular bisects the chord, attempting to use the full chord length 24 cm in Pythagoras.
Subtracted $13 - 12 = 1$ or arithmetic error.
Correct distance derived from the fundamental Pythagorean circle bisector theorem.
Confused distance with diameter quotient $24 / 4 = 6$.
Mistook $(13 - 6) = 7$ as the perpendicular leg.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge

Drop a perpendicular $OM$ from centre $O$ to chord $AB$. What does $OM$ do to segment $AB$?

Tier 2 · Tactical Step

The perpendicular bisects $AB$, so $AM = MB = \frac{24}{2} = 12\text{ cm}$. In $\triangle OMA$, hypotenuse $OA = 13\text{ cm}$.

Tier 3 · Crux Move

The perpendicular from the circle centre to a chord bisects the chord, forming a right triangle with the radius.

Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: B ($5\text{ cm}$)
Formal Verification Verified Answer: Option B ($5\text{ cm}$)

Step-by-Step Derivation:

1. Let $OM \perp AB$ where $M$ is the foot of the perpendicular on $AB$. 2. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord: $AM = \frac{1}{2} AB = \frac{24}{2} = 12\text{ cm}$. 3. Connect $O$ to $A$ to form right-angled $\triangle OMA$ with hypotenuse $OA = r = 13\text{ cm}$. 4. By the Pythagorean theorem: $OM^2 + AM^2 = OA^2 \implies OM^2 + 12^2 = 13^2$. 5. $OM^2 = 169 - 144 = 25 \implies OM = 5\text{ cm}$.
Verification Check:
Check chord length from distance: $AB = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2} = 2\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 2\sqrt{169 - 25} = 2\sqrt{144} = 2 \times 12 = 24\text{ cm}$. Matches stem exactly.

Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux

Connect both ends of the chord to the circle's centre. What type of triangle is formed by the two radii and the chord?

Tier 2 · Tactical Step

All three sides equal $r$, so the central angle is $60^\circ$. The angle subtended at the circumference is half the central angle.

Tier 3 · Crux Move

A chord equal in length to the radius forms an equilateral triangle with the center, subtending 60 degrees at the center and 30 degrees at the circumference.

Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: A ($60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$)
Formal Verification Verified Answer: Option A ($60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$)

By the Inscribed Angle Theorem, the angle subtended by an arc at the circumference is half the angle subtended at the centre. 5. For any point $P$ on the major arc: $\angle APB = \frac{1}{2} \angle AOB = \frac{1}{2}(60^\circ) = 30^\circ$.
Verification Check:
The inscribed angle on the minor arc would be $180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$. Reflex $\angle AOB = 360^\circ - 60^\circ = 300^\circ$, and $\frac{1}{2}(300^\circ) = 150^\circ$, confirming consistency.

Cyclic Quadrilateral Opposite Angle Bisectors Invariant

Subtopic: Cyclic Quadrilateral Angle Bisectors
Let $ABCD$ be a cyclic quadrilateral. The internal angle bisectors of opposite angles $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ intersect the circumcircle at points $P$ and $Q$ respectively. What can be concluded about the line segment $PQ$?
Commit Initial Reasoning & Working (Prior to Assistance)
Tempting Mistake & Why It Fails: Guessing parallelism to diagonals rather than using the $90^\circ$ diameter invariant.
Assumed chord length equals radius $R$ instead of $2R$.
Correct: $PQ$ is a diameter because the inscribed angle subtending it is $90^\circ$.
Parallelism to $BD$ only holds in special symmetric configurations.
Perpendicularity to $AC$ does not hold in asymmetric cyclic quads.
Hint Ladder (3 Progressive Steps) Nudge · Tactical · Crux
Tier 1 · Conceptual Nudge

Express the measure of arc $PQ$ in terms of the angle bisectors of $\angle A$ and $\angle C$.

Tier 2 · Tactical Step

$\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ \implies \frac{1}{2}\angle A + \frac{1}{2}\angle C = 90^\circ$. An inscribed angle of $90^\circ$ subtends a diameter.

Tier 3 · Crux Move

Since opposite angles of a cyclic quad sum to 180 degrees, the sum of their half-angles is 90 degrees, forcing the line joining the arc midpoints to be a diameter.

Model Derivation & Proof Correct Choice: B ($PQ$ is a diameter of the circle)
Formal Verification Verified Answer: Option B ($PQ$ is a diameter of the circle)

Step-by-Step Derivation:

1. In cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, opposite angles are supplementary: $\angle A + \angle C = 180^\circ$. 2. Dividing by 2: $\frac{1}{2}\angle A + \frac{1}{2}\angle C = 90^\circ$. 3. The bisector of $\angle A$ meets the circle at $P$, so arc $BP = \text{arc } PD$, meaning $\angle BAP = \angle DAP = \frac{1}{2}\angle A$. 4. The bisector of $\angle C$ meets the circle at $Q$, so arc $DQ = \text{arc } QC$, meaning $\angle DCQ = \angle BCQ = \frac{1}{2}\angle C$. 5. Connecting $P$ and $Q$ through a point on the circumference yields an inscribed angle subtended by chord $PQ$ equal to $\frac{1}{2}\angle A + \frac{1}{2}\angle C = 90^\circ$. 6. By Thales theorem, an angle inscribed in a semicircle is $90^\circ$, which implies chord $PQ$ subtends a semicircle and is strictly a diameter of the circle.
Verification Check:
Let $ABCD$ be a square (a cyclic quad with all angles $90^\circ$). Angle bisectors pass through the centre $O$, and $P, Q$ are diametrically opposite vertices. Thus $PQ$ is a diameter.

Distance between centres $O_1$ and $O_2$ is equal to radius $R = 5\text{ cm}$. The common chord $AB$ perpendicularly bisects segment $O_1 O_2$.

Tier 2 · Tactical Step

When two equal circles pass through each other's centres, the line of centres equals the radius and is bisected by the common chord.

The total length of the common chord is $AB = 2 \times AM = 2 \times \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$.
